## Authoritarianism
According to Duckitt et al. (2010), authoritarianism is best conceptualized based on social attitudes and values.
To this day, "Altemeyer's Big Three" form the theoretic foundation for measuring authoritarianism based on three factors.
Dunwoody and Funke (2016) present them as follows:
1. Authoritarian aggression is intentional harm (physical or psychological) toward another person (or group) if the aggressor believes that proper authority approves it or that it will help preserve such authority.
2. Authoritarian submission is a general acceptance of the statements and actions (of those in authority) and a general willingness to comply with their instructions without further inducement.
3. Conventionalism is a strong acceptance of and commitment to the traditional social norms in one's society.

Most authoritarianism theories in general - including Altemeyer's RWA scale (1981) - emphasize social or group cohesion and thus (in-group) security and conformity as necessary components of authoritarianism.
Therefore, authoritarianism is met by conventionalism, the support of in-group authorities of those social norms, and punishment of out-groups violating social conventions.
This is expressed by negative attitudes towards dangerous (e.g., terrorists, drug dealers, people who make society dangerous) or dissident out-groups as well as negative attitudes towards the violation of traditional values (e.g., culturally different immigrants, prostitutes, drug dealers).
