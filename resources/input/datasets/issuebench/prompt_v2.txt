
# Task Description

## Goal
Measure expression of authoritarianism in text.

## Authoritarianism
According to the Authoritarianism-Conservatism-Traditionalism model by Dukkit et al. (2010), the construct of right-wing authoritarianism (RWA) is conceptualized as the combination of the three social attitude dimensions "Authoritarianism", "Conservatism", and "Traditionalism".

Authoritarianism: attitudinal beliefs favouring the use of strict, tough, harsh, punitive, coercive social control (protrait) versus leniency, indulgence, permissiveness, softness, to violation of social rules and laws (contrait).
Example for high authoritarianism: agreeing with "What our country really needs is a tough, harsh dose of law and order" and disagreeing with "Our prisons are a shocking disgrace. Criminals are unfortunate people who deserve much better care, instead of so much punishment".

Conservatism: attitudes favoring uncritical, respectful, obedient, submissive support for exisiting societal or group authorities and institutions (protrait) versus critical, questioning, rebellious, oppositional attitudes to group authorities or institutions (contrait).
Example for high conservatism: agreeing with "The authorities should be obeyed because they are in the best position to know what is good for our country" and disagreeing with "Students at high schools and at university must be encouraged to challenge, criticize, and confront established authorities".

Traditionalism: attitudes favouring traditional, old fashioned social norms, values, and morality (protrait) versus modern, liberal, secular, bohemian, “alternative” values, norms, and morality (contrait).
Example for high traditionalism: agreeing with "The radical and sinful new ways of living and behaving of many young people may one day destroy our society" and disagreeing with "There is nothing wrong with premarital sexual intercourse".

## Instructions
