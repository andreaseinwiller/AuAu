## Authoritarianism
Authoritarianism is best conceptualized based on social attitudes and values.
Most authoritarianism theories in general - including Altemeyer's RWA scale (1981) - emphasize social or group cohesion and thus (in-group) security and conformity as necessary components of authoritarianism.
To this day, "Altemeyer's Big Three" form the theoretic foundation for measuring authoritarianism based on three dimensions. Dunwoody and Funke (2016) present them as follows:

### Authoritarian aggression ("aggression")
Authoritarian aggression is intentional harm (physical or psychological) toward another person (or group) if the aggressor believes that proper authority approves it or that it will help preserve such authority.
Authoritarian aggression is met by punishment of out-groups violating social conventions.
Authoritarian aggression is expressed by negative attitudes towards dangerous out-groups (e.g., terrorists, drug dealers, people who make society dangerous).

### Authoritarian submission ("submission")
Authoritarian submission is the general acceptance of the statements and actions of those in authority and a general willingness to comply with their instructions without further inducement.
Authoritarian submission is met by the support of in-group authorities.
Authoritarian submission is expressed by negative attitudes towards dissident out-groups.

### Conventionalism ("conventionalism")
Conventionalism is strong acceptance of and commitment to the traditional social norms in one's society.
Conventionalism is met by the support of in-group authorities of those social norms.
Conventionalism is expressed by negative attitudes towards the violation of traditional values (e.g., culturally different immigrants, prostitutes, drug dealers).
